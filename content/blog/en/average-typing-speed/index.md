On a computer, most people type between 30 and 60 words per minute (WPM), about 52 on average. That is around 260 characters per minute. If you manage 70 WPM or more, you are clearly faster than most people. On a smartphone the average is about 36 WPM.

Here you will find where these numbers come from, how to convert WPM into characters, and how to measure your own speed so that the number actually means something.

## Where the numbers come from

The largest study of typing on computers comes from Aalto University and the University of Cambridge. The team analysed [136 million keystrokes from 168,000 volunteers](https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/136Mkeystrokes/) who took an online typing test. The main results:

- On average, participants typed **52 WPM**.
- Most were between **30 and 60 WPM**.
- The fastest reached about **120 WPM**.
- According to the study, trained typists in the 1970s and 1980s reached 60 to 90 WPM.

A follow-up study by the same team with 37,000 volunteers measured [typing on smartphones](https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/typing37k): **36 WPM** on average, about 70 percent of the speed on a real keyboard. The fastest were 10 to 19-year-olds.

## Converting WPM to characters

In typing tests a "word" is not a real word but five characters, spaces included. That makes texts with long and short words comparable. The conversion is therefore simple: **characters per minute = WPM × 5**.

| WPM | Characters per minute | Characters per second |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 100 | 1.7 |
| 30 | 150 | 2.5 |
| 40 | 200 | 3.3 |
| 52 (average) | 260 | 4.3 |
| 60 | 300 | 5 |
| 80 | 400 | 6.7 |
| 100 | 500 | 8.3 |

Some tests count slightly differently, for example a capital letter as two keystrokes because Shift is pressed too. So only compare numbers from the same test.

## Where do you stand?

The following ranking is our rough guide, based on the numbers above. It applies to a real keyboard and an accuracy of at least 95 percent.

| Speed | Ranking |
| --- | --- |
| under 25 WPM | Starting out. Often still searching or looking at the keys. |
| 25 to 40 WPM | Solid basics, but still below average. |
| 40 to 60 WPM | Average. Good enough for most everyday tasks. |
| 60 to 80 WPM | Fast. Typing hardly slows your thinking down any more. |
| 80 to 100 WPM | Very fast, the level of practised professionals. |
| over 100 WPM | Top range that few people reach. |

A good everyday goal is 50 to 60 WPM with high accuracy. That puts you above average, and hardly any time is lost between your head and the screen.

## What the numbers don't tell you

**The study is not a cross-section of the population.** People who voluntarily take a typing test online probably type more than average. 68 percent of participants came from the US and typed English texts. In other languages, accented letters or keys at the edge of the keyboard can pull the speed down a little.

**Copying is faster than composing.** Typing tests give you the text. When you write down your own thoughts, thinking often sets the pace, not your fingers. Your test score is your upper limit, not your everyday speed.

**Speed without accuracy is worth little.** Many tests only subtract errors that remain at the end. What you fix right away costs time but doesn't show up in the score. We calculate how much that matters in [Accuracy before speed](/blog/precision-before-speed/).

## What fast typists do differently

The study also looked at how fast and slow typists differ. Two results stand out:

- **Fast typists make fewer mistakes.** Slower typists lose a lot of time noticing and correcting errors. The researchers sum it up like this: the key to fast typing is executing the movements correctly.
- **Fast typists overlap their keystrokes.** They press the next key before releasing the previous one. For them, this applied to 40 to 70 percent of keystrokes. It only works with well-practised letter sequences and when your eyes don't need to be on your fingers.

One surprising result: people who had taken a typing course were on average no faster than others. You can type fast with fewer than ten fingers, too. Touch typing still has advantages: you don't have to look at the keyboard, every key has a fixed finger, and that is exactly what makes smooth, overlapping keystrokes easier. A course on its own isn't enough, though. What counts is the practice afterwards.

## How to measure your speed properly

- **Type for at least one minute.** At 15 seconds, a single mistake decides the result.
- **Do several runs.** Take the middle value, not the best one.
- **Use texts in your language.** With capitals and punctuation, the way you write every day.
- **Look at accuracy.** A speed at 90 percent accuracy can't be compared with one at 98 percent.
- **Always measure under similar conditions.** Same keyboard, same time of day, not warmed up one day and cold the next.
