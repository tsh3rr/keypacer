There are many free typing trainers, and at first glance they look alike: text appears, you type it, and at the end there is a number. The difference lies in **what** you practise next. Some trainers give everyone the same course, others follow your mistakes, and others only measure your speed.

This article comes from Keypacer itself. We have tried to describe the other trainers fairly, and we also say when they are the better fit.

## The trainers at a glance

| Trainer | Approach | Adapts to your mistakes | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keypacer | A course that builds up key by key and creates every drill from your slowest keys and letter pairs | yes, by errors and time per keystroke | learning to touch type and getting faster in a targeted way |
| keybr | New letters join once the previous ones are fast enough, practice uses made-up words | yes, by speed per letter | starting with a few keys and lots of repetition |
| TIPP10 | Lessons in a fixed order, characters with many errors come up more often | yes, by errors | school and office, also as a program to install |
| Monkeytype | A typing test with many settings, not a course | no | measuring speed and comparing with others once you already touch type |
| TypingClub | A guided course with many short lessons and game elements | hardly, the path is basically the same for everyone | children and school classes |

## What "adaptive" really means

Almost every trainer counts errors. What matters is what it does with them.

- **Fixed course:** Everyone gets the same lessons. At most, errors decide whether you have to repeat a lesson.
- **Weighting errors:** Characters you often mistype come up more often. TIPP10 works this way, for example.
- **Speed per key:** The trainer measures how long you take for each letter and holds back progress until the slow ones are solid. keybr works this way.
- **Keys and letter pairs:** Keypacer times every keystroke to the millisecond, including the step between two letters. Often the problem is not the <kbd>q</kbd> alone but the move from <kbd>q</kbd> to <kbd>u</kbd>. The next drill then consists of real words with exactly those pairs.

## Real words or made-up ones?

keybr builds drills from made-up words that only contain the letters you have already learned. That lets you practise a lot even with just a few keys. Keypacer and TIPP10 use real words and sentences. That feels closer to everyday typing, because your fingers learn the common building blocks of your language.

## Language and keyboard layout

If you type in a language other than English, your layout matters. Keypacer recognises your layout from the keys you press and lets you practise in eight languages, on QWERTY, QWERTZ, AZERTY, Dvorak, Colemak and more. keybr and Monkeytype also offer many languages and layouts. TIPP10 comes from Germany and is designed for the German layout.

## Which trainer suits you?

- **You want to learn touch typing from scratch:** Keypacer, keybr or TIPP10. Try two of them for a few days and stick with the one you enjoy opening.
- **You already touch type and want to get faster:** Keypacer shows you which keys and pairs slow you down. Monkeytype is good for measuring your progress.
- **You practise with children or a class:** TypingClub and TIPP10 are built for that.
- **You want to practise without an account, ads or cookies:** Keypacer runs right in your browser and saves your progress there. An account is optional and only there so you can continue on other devices.

## Conclusion

There is no single best typing trainer. Short, regular sessions and [accuracy before speed](/blog/precision-before-speed/) matter more than the tool. But if you don't want to guess what to practise, a trainer that measures your weak spots and works on them directly helps.
