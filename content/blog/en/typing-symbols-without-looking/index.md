On a US keyboard you type the @ sign with <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd>, the hash with <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>3</kbd> and curly braces with <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>]</kbd>. On a UK keyboard some of them move: there the @ is <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>'</kbd>. On a Mac with a US layout, the symbols sit in the same places.

You need these characters all the time: in email addresses, prices, formulas and code. If you have to search for them every time, you lose your rhythm. Here is where they are, which finger types them and how to make them stick.

## Shift goes with the other hand

On a US keyboard almost every symbol is on the Shift layer of a number or punctuation key. The touch typing rule: **the other hand always presses Shift.** For <kbd>@</kbd> (<kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd>, left hand) you hold the right Shift key with your right pinky. For <kbd>&</kbd> (<kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>7</kbd>, right hand) you hold the left Shift key with your left pinky.

## All symbols at a glance (US layout)

| Symbol | Keys | Finger for the key |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>`</kbd> | left pinky |
| ! | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>1</kbd> | left pinky |
| @ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd> | left ring finger |
| # | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>3</kbd> | left middle finger |
| $ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>4</kbd> | left index finger |
| % | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>5</kbd> | left index finger |
| ^ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>6</kbd> | right index finger |
| & | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>7</kbd> | right index finger |
| * | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>8</kbd> | right middle finger |
| ( ) | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>9</kbd> and <kbd>0</kbd> | right ring finger and pinky |
| _ + | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>-</kbd> and <kbd>=</kbd> | right pinky |
| [ ] | <kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>]</kbd> | right pinky |
| { } | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>]</kbd> | right pinky |
| \ | <kbd>\\</kbd> | right pinky |
| \| | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>\\</kbd> | right pinky |
| : " | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>;</kbd> and <kbd>'</kbd> | right pinky |
| < > ? | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>,</kbd> <kbd>.</kbd> <kbd>/</kbd> | right middle, ring, pinky |

The ^ on the 6 key belongs to the right index finger in touch typing, even though it sits in the middle. The left index finger already covers 4 and 5.

## What is different on a UK keyboard

| Symbol | US | UK |
| --- | --- | --- |
| @ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd> | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>'</kbd> |
| " | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>'</kbd> | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd> |
| # | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>3</kbd> | own key next to Enter |
| £ | not on the keyboard | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>3</kbd> |
| € | not on the keyboard | <kbd>AltGr</kbd> + <kbd>4</kbd> |
| ~ | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>`</kbd> | <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>#</kbd> |
| \ \| | key above Enter | key left of <kbd>Z</kbd> |

<kbd>AltGr</kbd> sits right of the space bar and is held with the right thumb. On Windows, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Alt</kbd> works the same way.

## The pinky does a lot

On the right side, your pinky covers ten or more keys: brackets, braces, backslash, quotes, the minus and equals keys. That is why symbols there feel harder than letters. Two things help:

- **Move the hand, not just the finger.** For keys in the top row, shift the whole hand up a little instead of stretching the pinky as far as it goes. Then return to the home row.
- **Keep F and J as your anchor.** After every symbol, feel for the bumps on <kbd>F</kbd> and <kbd>J</kbd> again before you continue.

## How to learn symbols without looking

1. **The path first, then speed.** Type one symbol ten times in a row slowly and quietly say the combination, for example "Shift two". Only look the first time.
2. **Practise in real contexts.** An @ almost always appears in an email address, a bracket rarely on its own. So type `name@example.com` or `if (x) { y }` instead of single characters.
3. **Practise pairs together.** Opening and closing brackets sit next to each other. Once `{` is secure, `}` follows quickly.
4. **A few symbols at a time.** Take on two or three new symbols a day. Short daily repetition does more than one long session.
5. **Don't look at the keys.** If you don't know where a symbol is, look at the table instead of the keyboard. That way your head learns the position, not the glance down.
